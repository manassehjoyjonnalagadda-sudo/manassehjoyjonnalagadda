03 / METHODA PRACTICAL PERFORMANCE FRAMEWORK

FROM COURT
OBSERVATION TO
BETTER ACTION.

Good coaching connects what we see in practice with what the athlete needs to do under pressure. The framework below is a starting point—not a one-size-fits-all formula.

01◎

OBSERVE

Watch movement, contact point, recovery position, shot choices and the context around each rally.

NOTICE THE PATTERN ↗
02⌁

ANALYSE

Identify the likely limiting factor. Separate a technical issue from a timing, decision-making or physical-capacity issue.

ASK THE RIGHT QUESTION ↗
03↗

INTERVENE

Choose a clear cue, constraint or drill that targets the priority without overloading the athlete with instructions.

KEEP THE TASK PURPOSEFUL ↗
04◷

REVIEW

Check whether the change transfers to a more representative rally or game situation. Adjust based on the response.

RETEST IN CONTEXT ↗

Sports science note: training decisions should be adapted to the athlete’s age, training history, goals, readiness and clinical guidance where relevant.

TECHNICAL / 001↗
MOVEMENT · FOOTWORK

The split step is a timing skill—not just a jump.

Explore how opponent cues, timing and the first directional push affect court coverage.

TACTICAL / 002⌖
TACTICS · DECISION-MAKING

Every shot should solve a problem.

Look beyond winners: consider balance, opponent position, recovery and the next likely reply.

PERFORMANCE / 003∿
TRAINING · LOAD

Quality first. Fatigue is not the objective.

Consider the purpose of a drill, movement quality, rest intervals and the athlete’s response.
